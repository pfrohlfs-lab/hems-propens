Market: crowded among HEMS, early in households01 →

Among HEMS, PV surplus and dynamic tariffs are standard. In households they are not: only 5.9% of metering points have a smart meter. Whoever is ready when the rollout picks up is early.

gridX: scale, carried by E.ON03 →

White-label platform for utilities and device makers, over 240,000 connected assets in 15 countries. €16.1m loss in 2024; E.ON's loans were apparently turned into equity.

Consolinno: the fuse-box device03 →

DIN-rail energy manager with control box, sold to installers via wholesale for about €500 to €800. Strong on regulation, weak app (2.9 stars), equity used up.

Features: nearly identical05 →

26 and 29 of 36 features. Which vendor looks best depends on the weighting: gridX for utilities, Consolinno for installers, our concept for power users.

Apps: both show the present06 →

gridX shows a live flow and costs with vs. without HEMS, Consolinno live tiles in watts. Neither shows what the system plans next or why.

Our gap: power and heat, technology-open07 →

We optimise power and heat together and, given enough data, any device: heat pump, buffer, battery, EV, CHP. The schedule is the main view and can be shared with the grid operator.

Adopt from the competitors08 →

gridX's cost balance “with vs. without HEMS”, its solar priority list and push notices; Consolinno's charging target by departure time. Plus our own “Why?” per plan block.

Where we could go09 →

Four niches to keep open: heat-pump homes with buffer, hybrid heating and CHP, grid operators, and an open optimiser on top of existing systems.

Business model10 →

E.ON halves its subscription to €4.99 and makes Monitoring and Comfort free. Against that, our schedule would have saved the reference house €4,281 in 2025: the value lies in the optimisation, not the app fee.

In households, dynamic tariffs are not standard yet,the smart meter is the bottleneck.

Almost every HEMS can use a dynamic tariff, but only 5.9% of German metering points have the smart meter it needs. The rollout is speeding up (765,000 new smart meters in the first half of 2026), so being ready now means being early.

  • For mandatory installations the quota is already 26%; the law targets 90% of them by 2032.
  • Heat pumps are the growth market: 299,000 sold in 2025, 55% of all new heating systems in the first half of 2026.
  • CHP is a small niche: under 1% of households (our estimate), but with high value per home.

USP, strengths and weaknessesas a basis for the SWOT.

Short version per vendor for the presentation, including business model and customer group.

gridX

USPA ready-made, compliant HEMS as white label: partners offer their customers a HEMS without building it themselves and can market the assets as a virtual power plant.

Customers

B2B: utilities, device makers, installers and metering operators, often larger companies operating across Europe without their own development.

Strengths

  • White label: the framework is in place, partners put their brand on it
  • Vendor-independent, as far as devices are integrated or certified
  • International, built for different legislation
  • E.ON in the background strengthens creditworthiness
  • E.ON sells it as freemium: Monitoring free, Comfort with up to €420 bonus per year
  • Fast: installation possible in under 10 minutes
  • ISO 27001 certified since November 2026

Weaknesses

  • Optimisation logic not transparent, forecast quality not publicly proven, planning mainly price-based
  • Municipal utilities may be reluctant to share customer data because of E.ON
  • €16.1m loss in 2024, revenue not published
  • No joint planning of power and heat

Consolinno

USPEnergy manager and control box in one DIN-rail device: the installer meets §14a in the fuse box, and control runs locally without cloud.

Customers

Installers and contractors via wholesale, single-family homes behind them. For commercial and industrial sites, its own CEMS product.

Strengths

  • Broad regulatory coverage: §14a, §9 EEG, variable grid fees, ISO 27001
  • Local, no cloud, high data security
  • One-off price without subscription, free app with demo mode
  • Charging modes with a target charge by departure time

Weaknesses

  • Negative equity at the end of 2024, financed by bank and shareholder loans
  • Poorly rated app: 2.9 stars in the App Store
  • No savings in euros, heat only via SG Ready

Flexibility Manager

USPTechnology-open: given enough data, we optimise any device, and power and heat together. The 48-hour schedule is the main view and can be shared with the grid operator.

Customers

Still open, see “Where we could go”: heat-pump homes, hybrid heating and CHP, grid operators, or users of existing systems.

Strengths

  • Power and heat in one model: heat pump, buffer, battery, EV and CHP
  • MILP with four objectives instead of fixed rules
  • Copilot and autopilot as levels of autonomy
  • Explanation per plan block and visible impact

Weaknesses

  • Prototype without its own hardware or device integration
  • No §14a control

Where we could go:four niches to keep open.

No decision yet. Each niche builds on the same strength, optimising power and heat together for any device, but targets a different customer.

Heat-pump homes with buffer

9% of households have a heat pump, and it is the fastest-growing heating system. Planning the buffer together with PV and tariff is exactly what gridX and Consolinno lack.

Size: large and growing

Hybrid heating and CHP

Under 1% of households, but high value per home: in the reference house our schedule would have saved €4,281 in 2025. Nobody else plans CHP, heat pump and buffer together.

Size: small, high value

Grid operators

With §14a and the smart meter rollout, grid operators need predictable loads. A shareable 48-hour schedule gives them that instead of ad-hoc dimming.

Size: few customers, many homes each

Open optimiser for existing systems

Many homes already have a HEMS or an open-source controller. A technology-open optimiser that only delivers the plan can sit on top, without new hardware.

Size: open, fits a non-commercial path
