Deep dive · gridX vs. Consolinno · as of 25 Nov 2026

Two opposite poles, one shared gap: neither plans a hybrid-heating home ahead

gridX is an E.ON-owned white-label platform that lost €16.1 m in 2024. Consolinno is an independent DIN-rail specialist with an equity ratio of 1.2 %. Neither optimises CHP, heat pump and buffer tank jointly, and neither shows the user a schedule. Consolinno's FlexA logic, announced for homes in its "HEMS Vision 2026" with 24–48 h schedules, is the closest threat to our lead.

1gridX is a scale platform that E.ON pays for

250,000+ connected assets, 55+ OEMs and a live flexibility pool of 16,000 EVs. Losses grew from €1.7 m (2020) to €16.1 m (2024), €44.7 m accumulated. Six top-level management changes since April 2023.

2Consolinno is a regulation-first hardware specialist on a thin capital base

HEMS and FNN control box in one patented DIN-rail device, sold through 28+ wholesalers without a subscription. Revenue €2.84 m (2024) covers 61 % of personnel cost; the 2025 unit plan was cut from 20,000 to about 9,500.

3Neither has our core today; FlexA could change that

Both steer by rules or price signals. No joint CHP + heat pump + buffer model, no schedule view, no plan for the grid operator. FlexA already sends schedules to CHP units in industry; the home version had not shipped by Release 2.1 (08/2026).

So what for us

We face two different kinds of competitor. Against gridX we compete for the utility or installer that would otherwise license a platform. Against Consolinno we compete for the installer's DIN-rail slot. A student prototype wins neither fight on scale, so our entry point has to be a function both lack.

So what for us

Contacts at gridX may change quickly, so any conversation should be anchored with the company, not one person. Consolinno releases every 1–3 months; we should re-check its release notes before each of our milestone presentations, because a FlexA home release would change our positioning.

3

gridX answers to one corporate owner; Consolinno rests on two private shareholders and public grants

E.ON bought gridX in two steps: a majority in 2021, the remaining shares in 2022. Consolinno never raised venture capital: its owners paid in €3.85 m, and public grants contributed more.

So what for us

A partnership with gridX is in practice a partnership with E.ON, and municipal utilities may avoid it for that reason. Consolinno's decisions sit with two shareholders, which makes it easier to approach but less able to fund a joint project. Its grant record shows it likes research consortia, which is a natural format for working with a university team.

So what for us

A white-label HEMS at €5–10 a month has not paid for gridX's cost base, and both companies now look to flexibility revenue for profit. For us that means the business case has to include a flexibility or grid-service revenue line. Consolinno is a credible hardware partner for a pilot, but not for a long-term service dependency until its 2025 accounts show new capital.

5

gridX is cloud-first with its own gateway; Consolinno is local-first on a DIN-rail box; both steer by rules or prices

Both stacks solve device integration and §14a. Neither documents a joint model of CHP, heat pump and buffer tank in the home product, and neither shows a plan to the user.

So what for us

Our lead is the optimisation layer: one mixed-integer model over 48 h for CHP, heat pump, buffer, battery and EV, with the plan as the start view. Both rivals already solve the device integration and §14a work that we lack. We should build on their hardware and interfaces instead of copying them.

6

gridX sells under other brands; Consolinno sells hardware through wholesalers; reported end-customer prices fall on both sides

gridX never prices to the end customer directly; E.ON Home is the visible example. Consolinno publishes no list price, so street prices come from shops.

So what for us

We cannot out-distribute 28 wholesalers or E.ON. The realistic paths are to sell the optimisation as a module to a hardware or platform owner, or to target installers of hybrid heating (CHP plus heat pump), a segment neither serves. Price anchors: €0–10 a month or about €600–900 one-off.
