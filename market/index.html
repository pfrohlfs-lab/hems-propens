Chapter 4
1

The market grows with heat pumps and new price rules; the money moves to flexibility

Heat-pump sales rose 55 % to 299,000 in 2025, and since 2025 every supplier must offer a dynamic tariff.

So what

Size our opportunity on the ~0.25 M smart-meter prosumers and the 2035 flex pool, not the headline HEMS count. Next step: a MaStR query for CHP units ≤50 kW in homes.

2

The field is crowded, consolidating and mostly unprofitable

No HEMS we checked plans combined heat and power or shares its plan with the grid operator; base functions are standard everywhere.

So what

Base functions are table stakes; our space is joint CHP, heat-pump and buffer scheduling, a 24–48 h plan and grid-operator visibility – open, but closing. Revenue should run through a tariff or flex partner, not homeowners directly.

3

gridX has scale, Consolinno the fuse box; neither plans ahead, which is our opening

So what

Partner with Consolinno on hardware, treat gridX as a channel risk and watch FlexA: 24–48 h schedules in homes would attack exactly our lead.

4

Our opening is a 48-hour heat-side plan the grid operator can see

Our cheapest schedule would have saved the reference house €4,281 in 2025 (our mock-up), many times what any HEMS costs a year.

Questions for our supervisor · 5 questions
  1. Which niche should the prototype prove first: heat-pump homes with a buffer tank (volume) or CHP hybrids like our reference house (best fit, under 1 % of homes, est.)?Decides what we optimise and simulate next.
  2. Who would you bet on as the paying customer: the homeowner, a utility or Stadtwerk, a heating OEM or the grid operator?Decides the price model and the interface we build.
  3. Would a grid operator take a 48-hour household plan in advance today, and in which format (§14a EMS route, EEBUS, a portal)?No source shows a grid operator buying such plans yet.
  4. Can we get API access to one platform (gridX, Kiwigrid or Heartbeat AI) to run our optimiser on top of it?Tests the plug-in route instead of building integrations.
  5. Should the next deliverable quantify the extra flex value of CHP plus buffer on the reference house?BCG gives values for battery, heat pump and EV only.
So what

Prove value, not integrations: price the 48 h plan in euros a year on the reference house and a heat-pump-with-buffer profile; take it to one utility or OEM and one grid operator. The lead lasts until FlexA for homes ships.

Method · research streams, fact-check, confidence levels

Most numbers come from eight research streams run on 25.11.2026 on top of our earlier competitor analysis (20.11.2026). A second agent re-opened the cited sources for every key claim and wrote its corrections back into the data. This page shows only corrected values and marks low-confidence values.

Research streams

  • Market size (Ch. 2) and regulation & trends (Ch. 3, 6)
  • Entrants & exits and company size (Ch. 4, 6)
  • Pricing history and business models (Ch. 1, 5)
  • gridX and Consolinno deep dives (separate page, Ch. 7)

Plus the hems-finder.org feature matrix (83 systems, 20.11.2026), annual accounts (Lobbyregister, Bundesanzeiger), company registers and company releases and our own calculation from the Energy-Charts API.

Adversarial fact-check

A separate agent re-read each primary document or cited page and recorded a verdict per claim in check_<stream>.md: confirmed, corrected, unverifiable or refuted. Corrections were written back into the data with a note. The only refuted claim (an Enpal tariff "from 19 ct/kWh") was removed. The web-search budget ran out during the checks, so some values rest on one source.

Confidence levels

High: primary source read by us (filings, BNetzA, BCG, statutes). Medium: reputable secondary source or vendor release. Low: snippet, single secondary article, company directory or our own derivation. Low values are drawn hollow, dashed or hatched, or labelled "est." or "unverified".

Data-quality caveats · 6 points
  • hems-finder entries are vendor self-declarations.Nobody audits them; gridX ticks every one of the first 34 features. Shares such as "94 % control a battery" describe claims, not tested functions.
  • Start-up figures are sparse.Company figures come only from annual accounts (Lobbyregister, Bundesanzeiger), filings, company registers and company releases. Most HEMS start-ups publish no accounts, so they appear without revenue or results, and a few headcounts rest on a register or press article (marked low confidence). Companies with known results are a sample, not the market.
  • Definitions of HEMS differ by a factor of about four.Berg Insight counts any PV + battery + app (~1.9 M in Germany, derived), BCG only systems that optimise against dynamic prices (~0.5 M). Figures from different definitions must not be added.
  • Price history has an observation bias.Before 2025 the dataset holds no verified paid HEMS subscription, so a 2019–2024 price trend would show what was archived, not how prices moved. E.ON's €4.99 tier (11/2026) is unverified.
  • Company accounts are partly second-hand.gridX's 2020–2024 losses come from group research with consistency checks; the Bundesanzeiger originals were not read. 1KOMMA5° revenue is the 2024 HGB figure.
  • Some numbers are our own estimates.The market funnel steps, the flex-pool crossover year (~2032), the €178–180 retail margin, the positioning scores and the niche scores are ours and labelled as such. 2026 values are year to date.
