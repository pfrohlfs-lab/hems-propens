German HEMS market · November 2026

Basic home energy management is turning into a free add-on; the open space is a 48-hour heat plan the grid operator can see

1

A HEMS is a stack of seven layers, and value is moving away from boxes and apps toward flexibility marketing

There is no single definition of a home energy management system. We use a working definition and map the value chain from device to installation: what each layer does, how it earns money and who keeps the margin. The same company often plays several layers.

Working definition

A HEMS measures, forecasts and controls at least two flexible assets in a home (PV, battery, heat pump, EV) against price or grid signals.

Market numbers differ by a factor of about 4 depending on which definition a source uses. Every exhibit names its definition.

So what for us

Our Flexibility Manager sits in the optimisation layer, where most pure plays lose money (Consolinno: −€1.3 M on €2.8 M revenue in 2024). Standalone app or gateway sales will not carry it. It needs a route into the flexibility and tariff layers: white-label to a utility or VPP that monetises the schedule, or a grid-operator use case built on the plan we can share ex ante.

2

The installed base is already large, but the money that matters is the flexibility pool, which grows from €10–20 M today to over €1 bn by 2035

Counts of installed HEMS range from 0.5 M to almost 2 M in Germany depending on the definition. Heat pumps, batteries, electric cars and smart meters grew in 2025; home-rooftop PV additions fell. The bottleneck is the smart meter: about 0.25 M German prosumers can use price-based optimisation today (own estimate, low confidence).

So what for us

Do not size our opportunity on the headline HEMS count. The relevant numbers are the ~0.25 M prosumers with a smart meter today (own estimate) and the flex pool BCG puts at €1.15–1.35 bn in 2035. Heat pumps are the fastest-growing asset (299,000 sold in 2025, +55 %), which supports our heat-side optimisation. Our CHP + heat pump + buffer niche is still unsized; the next step is a MaStR query for CHP units ≤50 kW in homes.

3

Regulation now supplies the price signals a HEMS needs, and wider daily price spreads raise the value of planning ahead

Since 2024 Germany has added controllable-device rules (§14a), dynamic tariffs for every supplier, time-variable grid fees and 15-minute day-ahead prices. Two big files are still open in November 2026: the EEG 2027 reform and the AgNes grid-fee system planned for 2029.

So what for us

The signals our 48 h optimisation needs now exist by law: a dynamic tariff from every supplier, Modul 3 grid fees and 15-minute prices. Wider spreads raise the value of a good schedule even as negative hours fall. AgNes (planned for 2029) and the end of the §14a transition at the end of 2028 move toward measured, grid-oriented control, which favours plans shared with the grid operator in advance. Risk to watch: the coalition deal on EEG 2027 keeps feed-in tariffs for PV <25 kW, which weakens the self-consumption push.

4

The field is crowded, consolidating and mostly unprofitable, and no HEMS we checked plans combined heat and power or shares its plan with the grid operator

We mapped 74 providers that sell a home energy management system (HEMS) in Germany, the feature claims of 80 vendors on hems-finder.org and the headcount and results of 26 companies. Market entry peaked in 2023–2024. Since 2025 the news has been insolvencies and takeovers. Base functions are standard everywhere, so a new product has to win on assets and services the market does not yet cover.

So what for us

Competing on base functions or integration breadth is a losing game: 94 % of vendors already claim PV-surplus control, gridX connects 250,000+ assets and Consolinno 200+ device brands. Our defensible space is the right-hand block of the feature heatmap, which hems-finder does not track: joint scheduling of CHP, heat pump and buffer tank, a 24–48 h plan as the start view, and a plan the grid operator can see in advance. Consolinno has announced 24–48 h schedules for homes, so this window is open but closing. Since we sit in the optimisation layer, where most pure plays lose money, our route to revenue should run through a partner that owns the tariff or flex relationship (utility, heating OEM or grid operator) rather than through homeowners directly.

5

Paid HEMS software settles at about 5 EUR a month, while utilities and tariff players give it away

We collected 37 German HEMS offers with dated prices from 08/2021 to 11/2026 and re-checked 41 of their 66 price points against the cited source. Stand-alone devices cost about 250 to 1,000 EUR once, software subscriptions 4 to 7 EUR a month. Whoever sells electricity or trades flexibility can afford to charge nothing for the HEMS itself.

So what for us

A stand-alone fee of 25 EUR a month is five times the median paid subscription (4.99 EUR) and 1.7 times the most expensive mass-market offer (1KOMMA5° at 14.99 EUR). At 300 EUR a year it would also absorb most of the 350 to 450 EUR flexibility value BCG estimates per house. We should price the household app near 5 to 15 EUR a month, or sell the optimisation to the party that earns the tariff margin and the flex revenue: a utility, an aggregator or the grid operator who receives our ex-ante schedule.

6

Storage makers and installers exit, while capital and flexibility volume concentrate on a few platforms

Since 2024 the German HEMS market has seen insolvencies of storage makers and installers next to large funding rounds for platforms that own the customer, the tariff and a virtual power plant. Most HEMS-relevant companies with published results still lose money. Ten trends explain where the value moves.

So what for us

Integration breadth is where platforms such as Heartbeat, Kiwigrid or gridX win, and a student prototype will not catch up there. Our opening is a specialised optimisation module: joint CHP, heat-pump and buffer scheduling over 48 hours, with a plan the grid operator sees in advance. It answers the simultaneity risk that price-driven HEMS create, and it can plug into the platforms via EEBUS and §14a instead of competing with them for households.

7

Our opening is a 48-hour heat-side plan the grid operator can see, and Consolinno is the only competitor moving toward it

We placed 16 products on sale, one announced product and our prototype on two axes: how many assets it optimises together and how far it shows and shares its plan. The platforms crowd into one cell. The cells for heat-side optimisation and grid-shared plans are empty except for our prototype and an announcement.

Questions for our supervisor

  1. Which niche should the prototype prove first: heat-pump homes with a buffer tank (volume) or CHP hybrids like our reference house (best fit, under 1 % of homes, est.)?Decides what we optimise and simulate next.
  2. Who would you bet on as the paying customer: the homeowner, a utility or Stadtwerk, a heating OEM or the grid operator?Decides the price model and the interface we build.
  3. Would a grid operator take a 48-hour household plan in advance today, and in which format (§14a EMS route, EEBUS, a portal)?No source shows a grid operator buying such plans yet.
  4. Can we get API access to one platform (gridX, Kiwigrid or Heartbeat AI) to run our optimiser on top of it?Tests the plug-in route instead of building integrations.
  5. Should the next deliverable quantify the extra flex value of CHP plus buffer on the reference house?BCG gives values for battery, heat pump and EV only.
So what for us

Stop adding integrations and start proving value: run the Flexibility Manager on the reference house and on a heat-pump-with-buffer profile, express the 48 h plan's value in euros per year, and take that number to one utility or heating OEM and one grid operator before the end of the semester. The advantage lasts only as long as Consolinno's FlexA for homes is not shipped.

Confidence levels

High: primary source read by us (filings, BNetzA, BCG, statutes). Medium: reputable secondary source or vendor release. Low: snippet, single secondary article, company directory or our own derivation. Low values are drawn hollow, dashed or hatched, or labelled "est." or "unverified".

Data-quality caveats

  • hems-finder entries are vendor self-declarations.Nobody audits them; gridX ticks every one of the first 34 features. Shares such as "94 % control a battery" describe claims, not tested functions.
  • Start-up figures are sparse.Company figures come only from annual accounts (Lobbyregister, Bundesanzeiger), filings, company registers and company releases. Most HEMS start-ups publish no accounts, so they appear without revenue or results, and a few headcounts rest on a register or press article (marked low confidence). Companies with known results are a sample, not the market.
  • Definitions of HEMS differ by a factor of about four.Berg Insight counts any PV + battery + app (~1.9 M in Germany, derived), BCG only systems that optimise against dynamic prices (~0.5 M). Figures from different definitions must not be added.
  • Price history has an observation bias.Before 2025 the dataset holds no verified paid HEMS subscription, so a 2019–2024 price trend would show what was archived, not how prices moved. E.ON's €4.99 tier (11/2026) is unverified.
  • Company accounts are partly second-hand.gridX's 2020–2024 losses come from group research with consistency checks; the Bundesanzeiger originals were not read. 1KOMMA5° revenue is the 2024 HGB figure.
  • Some numbers are our own estimates.The market funnel steps, the flex-pool crossover year (~2032), the €178–180 retail margin, the positioning scores and the niche scores are ours and labelled as such. 2026 values are year to date.
